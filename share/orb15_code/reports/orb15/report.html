5-stock 15-minute range breakout - 6-month analysis

124 trading days (2026-04-06 to 2026-10-01), 1-minute Upstox candles, RELIANCE, TMPV, HDFCBANK, ITC, ADANIENT. All results are simulated on history; nothing here is investment advice.

0. What was tested

  1. 09:15-09:30 is candle 1 and is ignored. High/low of candle 2 + candle 3 (09:30-10:00) define the range - it is only known at 10:00, so that is the earliest entry.
  2. From 10:00 the first time price trades above the high (buy) or below the low (sell) is the entry, filled at the level (or at the open if a bar gaps through it). One trade per stock per day.
  3. Baseline exit (my pick from the data below): stop at the opposite side of the range, no profit target, square off at 15:15.
  4. Every position is Rs 100,000 notional (Rs 1L capital x 5 MIS leverage / 5 stocks). Charges use the estimated Upstox schedule from src/orb15/costs.py (unverified). If one 1-minute bar touches both stop and target the stop is assumed first.

Interpretation note: you wrote "wait till 9:45" and "2nd and 3rd 15-min candle". The 3rd candle closes at 10:00, so I read it as "45 minutes after the open". Plain reading. Before costs the breakout has a small positive edge (+10.1 bps per trade). Charges take 8.3 bps of it, leaving +1.9 bps. A 95% interval for the true net average (resampling whole days) is -5.1 to +9.5 bps - it straddles zero, so 6 months cannot distinguish this from no edge. The shape is classic breakout: 41% of trades are stopped for -1R, most of the rest ride to 15:15 for about +1R on average, but only 42% of trades finish in profit after charges, so you have to be able to sit through a lot of losers. Price broke out on 613 of 620 stock-days (99%), so the strategy is essentially always in the market. Typical stop distance is the range width, about 0.58% of price. 41% of breakouts later trade back through the opposite side of the range before 15:15 - that is the false-breakout rate an opposite-side stop has to absorb.

Stop: the opposite side of the range is clearly the best; every tighter stop is worse because the first pullback after a breakout usually retests the level (a 0.25x-range stop is hit on 84% of trades vs 41% for the opposite-side stop). Target: adding a fixed target lowers expectancy - the winners are the trades that keep running, capping them at 1R or 1.5R raises the win rate but loses more than it gains.

4. Entry and structure decisions

4a. How to detect "the candle breaks the line" (net bps)

Entering the instant price touches the level is not better than waiting for a candle close - waiting for a 5-min close beyond the level plus a small buffer is marginally better, since it skips some wick-only probes. The differences (about 1-3 bps) are inside the noise, so choose on practicality: a stop-limit/market trigger order is easiest, a 5-minute close needs you or the bot to watch the bar.

This is the most important risk to the strategy. A breakout order is a market-type order in a moving price; even 1 bp of slippage per fill erases the net edge, 2 bps makes it clearly negative and 5 bps turns gross to zero. These five stocks are very liquid so 1-2 bps is plausible, but it has to be measured live before trusting the numbers.

7. Decisions and verdict

  1. Stop: opposite side of the 09:30-10:00 range (data-backed; every tighter stop is much worse).
  2. Target / RR: none - hold to 15:15. Fixed RR targets lower expectancy at every level tested. If you want a number, 2R gives up the least (net +0.8 vs +1.9 bps). Trailing stops, break-even stops and early square-offs do not improve it either.
  3. Entry: the trigger type (touch vs 5-min close) is within noise. A small buffer (0.05-0.10%) or 5-min close is slightly better in this sample (about +1-3 bps); choose whichever you can execute reliably.
  4. One trade per stock per day; no re-entry after a stop-out. Entries after 12:00 are rare (5%) and do not matter.
  5. Sizing: costs are 8.3 bps on Rs 1L positions, 5.9 on Rs 2L and 4.5 on Rs 5L (flat brokerage). Net only turns clearly positive at Rs 2L+ per position, which needs more leverage and carries a proportionally larger drawdown.
  6. Filters: nothing is proven. Candidates to forward-test: breakout agrees with the 10:00 trend, wide range, high breakout volume.
Verdict. The pattern you saw is real in the sense that it is gross-positive (+10.1 bps), but over 6 months it is not distinguishable from zero after costs (net +1.9 bps, 95% range -5.1 to +9.5) and it disappears with ~1 bp of slippage per fill. The recent months look much better, which may be luck or a trending regime. Before risking money: (1) measure real slippage with a handful of paper/1-lot trades, (2) forward-test the baseline for at least 2-3 months, (3) widen the sample (more stocks or a longer history) - 5 stocks over 124 days is only 613 trades.
